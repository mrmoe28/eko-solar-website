# The 7-Point Checklist: Re-Roofing a House With Existing Solar

*A field checklist for roofing crews — what to confirm before you touch a panel.*

---

## Before the crew shows up

**1. Identify the racking system.**
Photograph the rail/clamp brand before removal (IronRidge, Unirac, SnapNrack, etc). Torque
specs and reinstall method are brand-specific — guessing voids the mount warranty, not just
the roof warranty.

**2. Confirm who installed the system and whether they're still active.**
If the original installer is still in business, some manufacturers require them (or a
certified installer) to sign off on removal/reinstall to keep the panel warranty valid.

**3. Check the interconnection agreement.**
Utility-tied systems may need a permit or notice before disconnect/reconnect. Skipping this
can delay the homeowner's production credits, which becomes your callback.

## During removal

**4. Label every panel and rail position before pulling anything.**
Panels going back on the same string, same orientation. Relabeling on the fly is where
reinstall mistakes start.

**5. Re-flash penetrations to the racking manufacturer's spec, not your standard flashing.**
Standard boot flashing is rated for vents, not the load and seal pattern solar mounts need.
This is the #1 source of post-reroof leak callbacks on solar homes.

## Before reinstall

**6. Test the array before you leave the job, not after the invoice.**
A quick continuity/production check catches a loose MC4 connector or damaged panel while
your crew (or your subcontractor) is still on-site.

## Liability

**7. Get it in writing who's covering panel damage during the job.**
If your crew touches the array, your GL policy needs to actually cover solar equipment —
most standard roofing GL policies don't. If a subcontractor handles it, get their COI on
file before the truck rolls.

---

**If more than two of these are "we don't currently do that" — that's the point where most
roofing companies in metro Atlanta hand the panel work to a licensed solar sub instead of
carrying the liability themselves.**
